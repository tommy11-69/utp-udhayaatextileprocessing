#### Section 3 — Detailed Processing Stages

**Heading:** Understanding Each Processing Stage

Use numbered cards or expandable sections. Each item should include:
- Process name
- Short technical explanation
- Purpose of the process
- Relevant quality outcome
- Whether the process is conditional, where applicable

**1. Greige Fabric Receipt**

Raw woven fabric is received according to the order requirements.

Purpose: Establish the incoming fabric quantity and identify the material requiring processing.

**2. Greige Fabric Inspection**

The fabric is checked for visible defects, stains, holes, construction issues, width, and quantity.

Purpose: Identify defects and discrepancies before subsequent processing.

**3. Singeing**

Loose surface fibres are removed by controlled burning.

Purpose: Improve surface cleanliness and help achieve a smoother fabric appearance, particularly for printing and higher-quality finishes.

**4. Desizing**

Warp sizing agents are removed where present.

Purpose: Improve absorbency and prepare the fabric for wet processing.

**5. Scouring**

Natural waxes, oils, and other impurities are removed.

Purpose: Improve fabric cleanliness and wetting behaviour.

**6. Bleaching**

Natural colouring matter is reduced when a white or light-coloured base is required.

Purpose: Prepare the fabric for suitable dyeing or printing applications.

**7. Mercerizing**

Suitable cotton fabrics are treated under controlled conditions using concentrated caustic soda.

Purpose: Improve lustre, dye affinity, and certain dimensional properties.

**8. Washing and Neutralization**

Residual chemicals are removed or neutralized as required.

Purpose: Prepare the fabric for subsequent treatments.

**9. Preparation / Zero-Zero**

Include this stage only after the technical meaning used by the company and its appropriate position in the workflow have been confirmed.

Do not publish an invented technical definition.

**10. Solid Dyeing**

Fabric is dyed to the specified solid shade.

Purpose: Achieve the required colour and shade consistency.

**11. Printing**

Designs are applied using the appropriate printing technique.

Purpose: Produce the specified patterns, colours, and design appearance.

**12. Post-Dyeing / Post-Printing Treatments**

Washing, soaping, fixation, curing, or other required treatments are selected according to the process used.

Purpose: Help achieve appropriate colour development, fixation, and removal of unwanted residues.

**13. Stentering and Width Setting**

Fabric width and dimensional properties are controlled.

Purpose: Meet specified width and appearance requirements. Heat setting applies where suitable for the fibre composition.

**14. Finishing Treatments**

Finishing may include softening, calendaring, or other specified mechanical or chemical treatments.

Purpose: Achieve the required handle, surface appearance, and functional properties.

**15. Sanforizing / Shrinkage Control**

Applied where required to achieve specified dimensional stability.

Purpose: Control residual shrinkage to meet buyer requirements.

**16. Final Inspection**

Finished fabric is examined for visible defects, shade variation, width, appearance, and order-specific requirements.

**17. Quality Testing**

Applicable tests may include colour fastness, dimensional stability, shrinkage, GSM, and other buyer-specified parameters.

Do not claim that every test is conducted on every order unless confirmed.

**18. Rolling and Measurement**

Approved fabric is rolled and measured according to the order specification.

**19. Packing**

Fabric is packed, identified, and labelled according to the required packing instructions.

**20. Dispatch**

Finished fabric is prepared for delivery according to the agreed order and delivery requirements.
